The Vanguard Jazz Orchestra began its life as the Thad Jones/Mel Lewis Orchestra in 1966.The world-famous Village Vanguard jazz club in New York City booked the newly formed band for three Monday nights that February. Those performances were wildly successful and the engagement was extended indefinitely. The Village Vanguard’s owner, Max Gordon, told Mel Lewis, “We’ll keep it going until it tapers off.” Over half a century and more than 2700 Monday nights later, it still hasn’t. The Vanguard Jazz Orchestra stands apart from all other modern ensembles of its kind and is still as strong and vital as ever.

LAST OF THE JAZZ JOINTS
AN INCOMPLETE HISTORY OF THE VILLAGE VANGUARD

The Village Vanguard is widely regarded as New York City’s oldest jazz club.

Virtually every major figure in jazz has descended the fifteen steps into the triangular basement at 178 Seventh Avenue South to perform on the Vanguard stage. The club’s intimate space and stellar acoustics create an environment uniquely suited to artistic innovation and close listening. This book pays tribute to those artists and to the remarkable body of recordings forged Live at the Village Vanguard.

The Vanguard is small and unassuming, yet it holds a rich history. This is not just the story of a jazz club, but of calypso, cabaret, and comedy-of folk music, Shakespeare, poetry, and perseverance. It is a New York story and a story of family, in every sense of the word

THE ANDY STATMAN TRIO

A truly extraordinary artist, Andy Statman began his career in the 70’s as a virtuoso Mandolinist who studied and performed with David Grisman, went on to study clarinet with the legendary Dave Tarras and became one of the main architect of a Klezmer revival that started out 30 years ago and has since informed and influenced folk, Jazz and improvised music forms.
